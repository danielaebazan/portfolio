I'm Dani.

Building with purpose, not just aesthetics.

I’m interested in what happens beyond the screen.

Over the past few years, I’ve worked with businesses and teams across different industries, building and improving websites, funnels, automations, and digital systems. That experience has taught me that a website rarely exists on its own. It’s part of a bigger journey that includes how people find a business, what they see, what they do next, and what happens behind the scenes.

That’s how I approach digital work today. Before building anything, I want to understand what it needs to accomplish, where things are getting stuck, and what should happen next.

Because good digital work isn't just about making something look good. It should make the business easier to understand, easier to navigate, and easier to move forward.

Let’s collaborate if you care about how the pieces fit together.

I enjoy working with people who care about the bigger picture and want to make thoughtful decisions before jumping into the next tool, page, or project.

Whether you're starting from scratch or trying to make sense of something that's already there, I like figuring out what's actually getting in the way and finding a practical way forward.

01

Clarity

I like knowing what something is supposed to do before figuring out how it should look. Clear goals and clear decisions make better digital experiences.

02

Systems thinking

My Industrial Engineering background taught me to look at how individual pieces work together. I bring that same mindset to websites, funnels, automations, and the processes behind them.

03

Collaboration

I’ve worked alongside business owners, marketers, designers, developers, and teams, and I’ve learned that the best solutions usually come from understanding the problem from more than one perspective.

04

Intentionality

I don't believe in adding things just because they are available. Every page, tool, automation, and decision should have a reason for being there.

Before moving into web development, I studied Industrial Engineering. It taught me to break complex problems into smaller pieces, understand how systems interact, and look beyond the most obvious solution. That way of thinking ended up becoming surprisingly useful in digital work.

Over the past few years, I’ve worked in professional environments across web development, design, marketing, and technology. I’ve built websites for businesses, worked alongside marketing teams, supported service businesses with their digital operations, and worked on everything from funnels and advertising to CRM systems and automations.

Those experiences gave me a much broader view of what happens behind a digital presence. I’ve seen the website side, the marketing side, and the systems side — and that’s a big part of why I approach projects as connected pieces rather than isolated deliverables.

And when I'm not building things, you’ll probably find me painting, baking, staying active, or finding some new creative project to obsess over for a while. I like having things outside of work that have nothing to do with websites or systems. They give me a chance to reset, stay curious, and come back to my work with a fresh perspective.
